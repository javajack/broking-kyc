> **Why this page is structured this way:** This segment is widely misunderstood in a specific way, and the misunderstanding has a number attached to it. So the page leads with the rule and the correction, because almost every operational decision in the segment follows from whether a client may hold an unhedged position — and the answer is not what the commonly quoted figure implies. The rest follows the usual shape: limits, settlement, controls.

<Aside type="danger" title="The correction that governs this whole segment">
Exchange-traded derivatives involving the domestic currency are for hedging **contracted exposure**. The commonly quoted figure — a dollar threshold often repeated as the amount a client may trade without demonstrating exposure — is **an evidence threshold, not a permission to have no exposure**. Beneath it a client need not *produce* the documentation; it does not follow that the client may hold a position without an underlying exposure existing.

**There is no unhedged allowance** under the hedging rule: for pure speculation without a qualifying underlying exposure, the permitted position is **effectively zero**. The rule is not a blanket statutory ban on retail participation, and a client with genuine contracted exposure may hedge it. But an unhedged speculative position does not satisfy the rule, and a broker whose onboarding treats the threshold as a free allowance is enabling positions the framework does not contemplate.

This is the single most consequential correction in this plane, and it determines the client declaration, the monitoring and the product's realistic addressable market.
</Aside>

## TL;DR

- **The rule is hedging of contracted exposure**, and the commonly quoted threshold is about **evidence**, not entitlement.
- **Position limits are structured by participant category** and expressed as the higher of a proportion of open interest or an absolute amount, with banks authorised to deal carrying far larger limits.
- **Limits linked to open interest apply at the time of opening a position** — a fall in total open interest does not force unwinding, but no increase or new position is permitted until compliant. **This is the origin of the passive-breach logic later imported into equity.**
- **An early-warning tier exists**: the clearing corporation notifies the clearing member when a client exceeds the limit **or exceeds a lower proportion of open interest**.
- **Positions beyond a stated size require a reporting file and an authorised-dealer or custodian confirmation.**
- **Settlement references the central bank's published rate**, so the settlement input is external to the exchange.

## 2. Position limits

Expressed as the **higher of a proportion of open interest or an absolute amount**, by participant category and currency pair.

| Participant | Shape of the limit |
|---|---|
| **Trading member, non-bank** | A proportion of open interest or a stated amount |
| **Trading member that is a bank authorised to deal** | The same proportion but a **far larger** absolute amount |
| **Non-bank broker, proprietary** | A proportion or a **smaller** amount than the member limit |
| **Client** | A **lower proportion** and a smaller amount |
| Qualifying foreign investor categories | Varying by category, with individuals, family offices and corporates on the lower tier |
| Combined across pairs | Proprietary limits also expressed as a combined cap across the domestic-currency pairs |
| Cross-currency pairs | Their own limits |

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| **Gross position definitions** | Long is long futures plus long calls plus short puts; short is short futures plus short calls plus long puts |
| **Timing of the open-interest test** | **Applies at the time of opening a position.** A fall in total open interest does **not** force unwinding, but the participant **cannot increase existing or create new positions** until compliant |
| **The early-warning tier** | The clearing corporation **notifies the clearing member when a client exceeds the limit or exceeds a lower proportion of open interest** — a genuinely useful signal most firms do not instrument |
| **Positions beyond a stated size** | The clearing member must report client details through a **prescribed format**, with an **authorised-dealer or custodian confirmation** |
| Non-resident participant codes | A request format and a bank letter, to named addresses |
| Interest-rate derivatives | Limits by **maturity bucket**, as a proportion of open interest or an absolute amount, differing for members, banks, primary dealers and fund managers against other clients |

## 3. Settlement and contract mechanics

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Contracts | Futures and options on the domestic currency against major currencies, plus cross-currency pairs |
| **Settlement price** | References the **central bank's published reference rate** — an input external to the exchange, which must be ingested rather than computed |
| Settlement | Cash settled against that rate |
| Margin | Through the segment's own parameter files, with their own snapshot cadence |
| Trading hours | The segment's own, differing from the equity session |
| Interest-rate derivatives | Listed instruments with their own settlement basis, traded predominantly by institutional participants |

## 4. Broker-side controls

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Client declaration of contracted exposure** | Collected at activation, with the nature of the exposure recorded — **not a tick-box against a threshold** |
| Documentation | Collected where the position requires it, and retained |
| **Monitoring against the declared exposure** | Positions compared with the exposure the client declared, not only with the limit |
| **The reporting file for larger positions** | With the authorised-dealer or custodian confirmation, before the position is permitted to grow |
| Limit enforcement at order entry | Against client and proprietary limits, by pair and combined |
| **The early-warning notification instrumented** | So the lower-proportion signal reaches the risk desk rather than a mailbox |
| Reference-rate ingestion | As a register entry with its own criticality, since settlement depends on it |
| Activation gating | The segment enabled only where the declaration and documentation framework exists — see [the product enablement matrix](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/products/09-product-enablement-matrix/) |
| Client communication | That the product is a hedging instrument, and what that means for their eligibility |

## 6. Practical notes

- **Write the client declaration around contracted exposure, not around a threshold.** A declaration that only asserts a position size below the threshold does not establish what the rule requires.
- **Monitor positions against the declared exposure.** A client within the limit but beyond their declared exposure is the case the rule is about.
- **Instrument the early-warning notification.** It arrives before the breach and most firms never route it anywhere.
- **Treat the reference rate as a critical register entry.** Settlement depends on an input the exchange does not produce.
- **Be realistic about the addressable market.** A product restricted to hedging contracted exposure has a different client base from a speculative product, and sizing it as the latter leads to an activation framework nobody uses.
