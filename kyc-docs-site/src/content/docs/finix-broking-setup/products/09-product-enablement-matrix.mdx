> **Why this page is structured this way:** Enabling a product is usually treated as a commercial decision with a technical dependency, and it is better understood as accepting a set of obligations. Each product in this plane adds permissions to obtain, documents to collect, systems to build, people to certify, reports to file and a residual risk to carry. So this page is one matrix across all of them, with the residual risk stated explicitly — because the residual risk is the part that gets discovered after launch rather than decided before it.

## TL;DR

- **Enabling a product enables an obligation.** The matrix lists the six things that must be true, and the sixth — the residual risk — is the one firms do not decide deliberately.
- **Several products need permission per exchange**, and approval at one venue is not approval at another.
- **Some products cannot be safely offered without a capability the firm may not have** — handling a commodity delivery residue, or funding a physical settlement obligation.
- **Certification is per function**, so a product can be blocked by a person rather than by a system.
- **Reporting obligations begin at enablement, not at first trade** — including nil reporting in at least two products.
- **The honest answer is sometimes not to enable.** A product whose residual risk the firm cannot carry should not be switched on because the demand exists.

## 1. The six tests

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Permission** | Does the firm hold the regulatory and exchange permission, per venue? |
| **Client documentation** | What must be collected, disclosed and acknowledged before a client can use it? |
| **System capability** | What must the order, risk, back-office and money systems be able to do? |
| **People and certification** | Which functions need which certification, and who is accountable? |
| **Reporting** | What begins to be owed from enablement — including nil reporting? |
| **Residual risk** | What is the firm accepting that it cannot fully control, and can it carry it? |

## 3. Enablement sequence

| Step | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Decide the residual risk is acceptable** — explicitly, at the level that can accept it |
| 2 | Obtain the permission, **per venue** |
| 3 | Build and test the system capability, including the **reporting** |
| 4 | Certify the people, and confirm the identifier entitlements |
| 5 | Prepare the client documentation and the interface disclosures |
| 6 | Obtain the software approval where the product needs one — see [the certification plane](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/certification/) |
| 7 | **Register the new reporting obligations in the calendar, including nil reporting** |
| 8 | Add the product's files to the file register and its day types to the manifest |
| 9 | Add the product's actions to the operations catalogues and its owners to the matrices |
| 10 | Enable for a controlled population first, with the monitoring in place |
| 11 | Review after a defined period against the residual-risk assumptions |

<Aside type="danger" title="Two products should not be enabled on demand alone">
**Compulsory-delivery commodity contracts** and **physically settled stock derivatives** share a structure: the firm's avoidance strategy works almost always, and when it fails the firm holds an obligation measured in the full value of the underlying rather than in margin. Enabling either without the ability to fund, take, store where relevant, and liquidate that residue is a strategy with a known failure mode and no mitigation.

The honest test is not "can we close client positions before the deadline" — that is the plan, and plans fail on illiquidity, price limits and absent bids, which are correlated with exactly the conditions that make the position dangerous. The test is **"if a residue of a realistic size survives our cut-off, can we carry it"**. A firm that cannot should offer cash-settled contracts in those commodities and should not enable the compulsory-delivery ones, and should record that decision rather than leaving it to be discovered in an expiry week.
</Aside>

## Practical notes

- **Decide the residual risk first.** Everything else is execution; this is the decision.
- **Register the reporting at enablement.** Two products owe nil reporting from the day permission is held, and a firm that waits for a first trade is already late.
- **Treat enablement as a change to five registers** — files, manifest, catalogues, matrices and the submissions calendar. A product enabled in the trading system and nowhere else is a product nobody is operating.
- **Enable for a controlled population and review.** The residual-risk assumptions are assumptions until some volume has tested them.
- **Record a decision not to enable.** It is as much a decision as enabling, and it prevents the question being reopened informally.
