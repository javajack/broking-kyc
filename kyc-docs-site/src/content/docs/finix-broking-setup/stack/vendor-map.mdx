> **Why this page is structured this way:** A vendor list is useless; a responsibility map is not. So the page is in four parts. First, what each product publicly claims, separated from what this manual assumes, so you can see where the evidence ends. Second, the capability matrix — one row per broking function, one column per product — because that is where overlaps and gaps become visible. Third, the overlaps, each of which needs a decision rather than a default. Fourth, the gaps: functions no product in this stack covers, which someone has to own anyway.

## TL;DR

- **Saral MoneyMaker is the order and risk layer.** Publicly: a multi-exchange dealer terminal for <abbr title="National Stock Exchange of India">NSE</abbr>, <abbr title="BSE Limited (formerly Bombay Stock Exchange)">BSE</abbr>, <abbr title="Multi Commodity Exchange of India">MCX</abbr> and <abbr title="National Commodity & Derivatives Exchange Limited">NCDEX</abbr> with <abbr title="Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk">SPAN</abbr> margining and a risk management system, plus browser and mobile clients, an algorithmic module with exchange-approved algorithm execution, and limit control at identifier, client and scrip level with margin computed before the order is allowed to flow to the exchange.
- **SecMark is the record and compliance layer, and also offers middleware that overlaps the order layer.** Publicly: back office for broking, depository participants, institutional desks and proprietary trading; a middleware and API suite doing order orchestration, real-time risk checks, margin validation, trade lifecycle and reconciliation; a partner portal for sub-broker hierarchies; SmartReco for reconciliation; C-Safe for screening against more than fifty lists including <abbr title="Politically Exposed Person">PEP</abbr>; <abbr title="Anti-Money Laundering">AML</abbr> Sutra for transaction monitoring and FINnet 2.0 reporting; and ComplianceSutra across six modules.
- **That overlap is the single most important decision in this stack.** SecMark middleware and Saral <abbr title="Risk Management System">RMS</abbr> can both perform real-time risk checks and margin validation. [Only one may own the release decision](#3-overlaps-that-need-a-decision), and this manual gives it to Saral RMS.
- **Digio is the identity, consent and signature layer** — Aadhaar and <abbr title="Permanent Account Number">PAN</abbr> verification, <abbr title="Optical Character Recognition.">OCR</abbr>, face match, DigiLocker, offline Aadhaar, Video <abbr title="Know Your Customer (process).">KYC</abbr>, <abbr title="Central KYC (records registry)">CKYC</abbr> and <abbr title="KYC Registration Agency">KRA</abbr> connectivity, Aadhaar eSign and certificate-based signing, eStamping, <abbr title="Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction">DDPI</abbr> and nomination document preview with digital stamp duty, and eNACH and <abbr title="Unified Payments Interface">UPI</abbr> AutoPay mandates.
- **CVL KRA is the primary KRA and is not an island.** Five KRAs are registered with <abbr title="Securities and Exchange Board of India">SEBI</abbr> and a mandated back-end interoperability framework makes them behave as one logical system: a query to any KRA returns the record whoever holds it, and a duplicate upload for an already-compliant investor is rejected rather than accepted.
- **<abbr title="Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India">CERSAI</abbr> <abbr title="Central KYC Records Registry">CKYCRR</abbr> is a different system with a different model, and it changed in 2026.** CKYCRR 2.0 went live on 1 August 2026 with real-time APIs, structured payloads, <abbr title="One-Time Password">OTP</abbr>-based consent for download and masked Aadhaar, replacing the batch-and-PDF model.
- **Razorpay is two products, not one.** The payment gateway and Smart Collect sit on the collection side; RazorpayX Payouts sits on the disbursement side with a contact, fund-account and payout object model and webhooks on every state change. They have different reconciliation semantics and must not share a code path.
- **Nothing in this stack clears, settles, custodies or lends.** Those stay with the clearing corporations, the depositories, the banks and — if the broker offers margin funding — the broker's own balance sheet.

## Conceptual overview

There is a temptation, when a stack has been chosen, to treat the vendor boundary as an architectural boundary. It is not. Vendors sell overlapping suites because that is how they grow, which means a broker who buys two suites has bought the same function twice and must decide which copy is authoritative. Where that decision is not made explicitly, it gets made implicitly — usually by whichever system happened to be integrated first — and the result is a stack where two components both believe they are enforcing a limit and neither is enforcing all of it.

The second thing a vendor map has to do is mark where the evidence stops. A great deal of what a broking stack does is not described on a public website, for good reasons: the integration kits are confidential, the screens change, and the configuration surface is customer-specific. This manual's approach is to state the public claim, then build the operational detail as a reference implementation that satisfies the regulatory requirement, and to keep the two visually distinct. The `[public]` and `[reference implementation]` markers in the tables below carry that distinction, and they are used consistently in every plane of this section.

The third thing is to be honest about gaps. The stack as specified has no treasury management system, no customer relationship or service-desk system, no data warehouse, no market-data vendor, and no general ledger for the firm's own books as distinct from client ledgers. Each of those is a real operational need. Leaving them unnamed in the brief does not make them disappear; it just means the broker will build or buy them later, under time pressure, which is the worst moment to choose.

<Aside type="caution" title="A broker is not an ordinary merchant">
Everything a payment aggregator does is built for merchant collection, where money received is the merchant's revenue. A broker's pay-in is not revenue — it is client money that must land in a designated client bank account, must be segregated from the broker's own funds by statute under regulation 18(2)(c) of the 2026 regulations, and is subject to the upstreaming regime. That changes which account the gateway settles into, how settlement timing is configured, and what the broker may do with the balance. The [money plane](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/money/) treats this as its central constraint rather than a footnote, and [pay-in rails](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/money/02-payin-rails/) covers the validated UPI handle regime that applies to broker collection specifically.

## 3. Overlaps that need a decision

Each of these is a place where two products in the stack can do the same job. None of them has a right answer independent of the broker's circumstances; all of them have a wrong answer, which is leaving it undecided.

### 3.1 Real-time risk checks: Saral RMS or SecMark middleware

Both claim it. Saral states that on order entry the trading system computes VaR and extreme loss margin and allows the order to flow within the available exposure limit; SecMark states its middleware performs real-time risk checks and margin validation as part of order orchestration.

**Finix decision: Saral RMS owns the release decision; SecMark middleware does not evaluate risk in the order path.**

The reason is not that one product is better. It is that the release decision must be made by exactly one component, because the component that makes it is the one that must be able to explain, for any historical order, why that order was or was not released. Splitting the decision across two systems means neither holds the complete explanation, and the first inspection that asks "show me why this order passed" becomes a reconstruction exercise. A secondary reason is latency: every additional hop in the order path before release is a hop that has to be available, monitored and capacity-planned.

SecMark middleware is still used — for order orchestration between channels and for the trade lifecycle and reconciliation functions on the return path, which is where its value is unambiguous. What it does not do is hold a veto.

**What this costs:** SecMark's margin validation would have given a second, independent computation of the same number, which is a genuine control. Finix recovers part of that by running SecMark's computation **off the order path, as a shadow check**, comparing it to Saral's number on a sampled basis and alerting on divergence. A divergence alert is an engineering defect report, not a trading halt. See [pre-trade check ladder](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/rms/03-pretrade-check-ladder/).

### 3.2 Client onboarding front end: Digio, SecMark e-KYC, or the broker's own journey

All three are available. SecMark markets an e-KYC product with face matching and liveness; Digio markets DigiKYC with the same capability set; and the broker has its own journey specified elsewhere on this site.

**Finix decision: the broker owns the journey and the user interface; Digio owns the verification, signature and registry calls; SecMark e-KYC is not used.**

The journey is where differentiation and conversion live, and it changes often — far more often than a vendor release cycle. The verification primitives are commodity, regulated, and expensive to build. Splitting at that line means the broker can redesign screen flow without a vendor dependency, and can swap a verification vendor without redesigning screens. Choosing *both* vendor e-KYC products would mean maintaining two sets of credentials, two audit trails and two sets of consent artefacts for the same regulatory obligation.

### 3.3 Screening: SecMark C-Safe or Digio AML

Both offer screening. C-Safe is described as covering more than fifty global and domestic lists including SEBI, UNSC, OFAC and PEP; Digio offers AML and counter-terrorist-financing screening.

**Finix decision: SecMark C-Safe is the system of record for screening; Digio screening is not enabled.**

Screening is not a one-time onboarding event. It has to re-run when a list changes, when a client's attributes change, and periodically for the whole book — and the results have to flow into transaction monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting. That lifecycle sits naturally with AML Sutra and ComplianceSutra in the same suite. Putting onboarding screening in one vendor and ongoing screening in another creates two populations of alerts with different list versions, which is indefensible in an inspection. See [PEP and sanctions screening](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/onboarding/04-pep-sanctions-screening/).

### 3.4 Reconciliation: SmartReco or the back office's own engine

SecMark offers both a back office and SmartReco as a separate reconciliation product.

**Finix decision: the back office performs trade and obligation reconciliation against exchange files; SmartReco performs bank, payment-gateway and depository reconciliation.**

The split follows the data. Trade-to-obligation reconciliation is intrinsic to the back office's own processing and cannot sensibly be externalised. Bank and gateway reconciliation involves high-volume, many-to-many matching against third-party statements with partial identifiers, which is exactly the problem a dedicated matcher is built for. See [bank reconciliation](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/money/04-bank-recon/).

### 3.5 Market data: exchange feed, Saral, or a third party

Saral references direct multicast. The exchanges sell data. The broker also needs historical data, corporate-action-adjusted series, and data for non-trading purposes such as collateral valuation and surveillance.

**Finix decision: exchange multicast feeds into Saral for trading and risk; a separate data store, sourced from exchange end-of-day files, serves valuation, surveillance, reporting and client-facing history.**

Risk and trading need the live feed and nothing else. Everything else needs a stable, corrected, replayable record, and using the live feed for it produces numbers that cannot be reproduced tomorrow. The valuation price used in a margin call must be reproducible in an inspection six months later; a tick from a multicast feed is not.

### 3.6 Client-facing mobile and web: Saral or SecMark

Both offer web and mobile trading front ends, and SecMark explicitly markets open APIs for integration with multiple OMS and RMS platforms.

**Finix decision: Saral Money Maker Browser and Handy at launch; revisit before the first major client-experience programme.**

At launch, a single vendor across terminal, web and mobile means one order-entry semantic, one set of order-type behaviours and one support path — which matters more in the first year than interface quality does. The decision is explicitly flagged for revisiting, because the front end is the component most likely to be replaced and SecMark's OMS-agnostic API posture is what makes a later replacement feasible.

## 5. Single-owner rule

Three things in this stack have exactly one owner, and the rule is worth stating as a rule because every plane depends on it.

| Thing | Owner | Why dual ownership fails |
|---|---|---|
| The order book | Saral OMS | Two order books means two answers to "is this order live", and the exchange only agrees with one of them |
| The margin number at the moment of release | Saral RMS | Two numbers means an order released against the lower one and reported against the higher one |
| The ledger of record | SecMark back office | Two ledgers means a client balance that depends on which screen you opened, and a contract note that cannot be defended |

Everything else in the stack may be duplicated, shadowed, cross-checked or reconciled. These three may not.

## Practical notes

- **Ask every vendor for the integration kit before signing, not after.** The kit tells you whether the published capability is an API, a file drop or a screen. That difference determines whether your integration is a week or a quarter, and it is not discoverable from marketing material.
- **Write down the overlap decisions in the contract.** If SecMark middleware is not doing risk checks, the statement of work should say so, because otherwise you will pay for the module and inherit the obligation to test it.
- **Version the list of lists.** C-Safe screening against "fifty-plus lists" is only meaningful if you know which fifty on the day a given client was screened. That version is evidence. See [PEP and sanctions screening](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/onboarding/04-pep-sanctions-screening/).
- **Treat the Pharos and Money Maker Challenge modules as approval-gated.** Anything that places orders without a human pressing a key is algorithmic trading, and it needs exchange approval before use. See [algo, API, DMA and <abbr title="Smart Order Routing">SOR</abbr>](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/oms/09-algo-api-dma-sor/).
- **Keep one page in your own wiki that maps vendor module to regulatory obligation.** When an inspection asks which system evidences a given paragraph, you want to answer in one minute, not one day. [Compliance configuration baseline](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/compliance/01-compliance-config-baseline/) is the template.
