> **Why this page is structured this way:** A broker setting up in 2026 inherits a rulebook that moved substantially during 2025 and 2026 — a complete replacement of the governing regulations, a new closing auction session, a new unpaid-securities regime, revised transaction taxes, a reorganised penalty framework, and a long tail of ease-of-doing-business changes that *removed* obligations. Documentation that lists only additions gets the workload wrong in both directions. So this page is a register organised by theme with the effective date and the required action on every row, a clearly fenced section for proposals, and a final section on the discipline that keeps it current — because a change register is a process, not a document.

<Aside type="danger" title="Proposals are fenced in section 11 and must stay there">
Several changes widely reported as current rules were **only proposed in consultation papers** as at 15 October 2026. The most consequential are in the funding facility, where press coverage has described proposed eligibility, funding sources and exposure arithmetic as though they were in force. **A firm that configures a proposal operates outside the framework while believing it is ahead of it.** Section 11 lists them. Nothing outside section 11 on this page is a proposal; nothing inside it should be configured.
</Aside>

## TL;DR

- **The governing regulations were replaced.** The 2026 broker regulations were notified on 7 January 2026 and repeal the 1992 regulations, with savings — so every registration, compliance and governance mapping has to be rebased, and new registration forms apply.
- **A closing auction session arrived in August 2026**, changing closing-price determination, the derivatives close time, order carry-forward, and the intraday snapshot regime around it. A reversal decision was pending at the time of research.
- **The unpaid-securities regime was revised**, with its main changes from 31 October 2026 and an exceptional extension phase from 3 January 2027.
- **Transaction taxes rose on 1 April 2026** for futures sales, option sales and exercised options, and the principal exchange's fee allocation changed on 1 March 2026 while the totals did not.
- **Obligations were removed as well as added** — demat reporting to exchanges, client cash-equivalent balance reporting, and a fallback platform for client risk reduction were all discontinued.
- **The technical-glitch framework was replaced** effective 9 January 2026, with reporting within two hours, a preliminary report the next day, and root-cause analysis within fourteen **working** days.
- **Investor-awareness messages became permanent from 1 November 2026** under a named programme, implemented separately on websites and applications.

## Conceptual overview

Regulatory change for a broker arrives through four different doors and each needs a different response. A **regulation** replaces the legal basis of the business and requires re-mapping rather than a configuration change — the 2026 regulations are of this kind. A **circular** typically changes a specific obligation with a stated effective date, and is the ordinary case. A **market-infrastructure change** — a new session, a new file format, a retired dissemination channel — changes the firm's technical environment whether or not the firm agrees, and its deadline is not negotiable. And a **consolidation** reissues existing material without changing it, which is dangerous precisely because it looks like a change and because a consolidation can carry forward text that a later circular has superseded.

The second observation is that 2026 was unusually bidirectional. A sustained ease-of-doing-business programme removed obligations: demat-account reporting to exchanges abolished, a letter-of-confirmation workflow replaced by direct credit, client cash-equivalent balance reporting discontinued, a risk-reduction platform discontinued, single filing across exchanges for a defined list. A compliance function that only ever adds rows to a calendar will be producing returns nobody wants and paying for effort nobody requires, while believing it is being thorough. Removal is as much a change-management event as addition, and it needs the same evidence trail — which is why the register in [periodic submissions](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/monitoring/03-periodic-submissions/) keeps an abolished list alongside the live calendar.

The third point is that a consolidated circular is not authority for the currency of everything in it. The June 2025 broker master circular predates the 2026 regulations, the discontinuation of the risk-reduction platform and much else; an April 2026 consolidated clearing circular still reproduces a timing sentence prefaced by a date that has passed; a common-submissions exhibit still lists an obligation abolished weeks after it was published. The operating rule is that **later operative text governs a conflict**, and that a firm must keep its own source and version history rather than relying on a consolidation to be self-consistent.

## 1. Governing regulations and registration

| Change | Effective | What the firm must do |
|---|---|---|
| **The 2026 broker regulations replace the 1992 regulations**, with savings | **Notified 7 January 2026**, effective on gazette publication | Rebase registration, compliance, code-of-conduct and governance mappings; preserve the savings provisions; update every internal citation |
| **A resident designated-director condition** | Existing brokers given **six months from notification** | Appoint or confirm at least one designated director resident for the prescribed part of the financial year; intimate through the exchange |
| **New registration forms** for brokers and clearing members | Deemed effective from the regulations' date | Use the prescribed forms with correct 2026-regulation references |
| **Fit-and-proper assessment amended** | **Gazette published 16 April 2026**, immediate | Update director and controller declarations and the escalation path. **Do not automatically equate an allegation with disqualification** |
| Turnover fees restated in the regulations | With the regulations | Re-verify the per-segment rates, including a **debt-segment rate that older summaries state differently** |

## 3. Market structure and settlement

| Change | Effective | What the firm must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Closing auction session, and modified pre-open** | **Auction 3 August 2026**; pre-open modifications **7 September 2026** | **Change order-system session rules, order carry-forward, risk handling and closing-price processing.** A reversal decision was pending at the time of research — re-verify |
| **Snapshot and ratio changes consequent on the auction** | Aligned with the auction rollout | A **fifth snapshot window** after the index equilibrium price; **no cure interval for late snapshots**; closing-auction orders excluded from the cash order-to-trade ratio |
| **Exchange-traded fund base price, bands, call auction and close-out norms** | Original date extended to **7 September 2026** | Update reference prices, order validation, and shortage and close-out processing. **Align the release to the revised date** |
| Single-window access framework for eligible foreign investors | **1 June 2026** | Institutional and designated-participant scope: classify eligible investors and follow the tailored cycle |
| Net settlement of funds for foreign-investor cash-market transactions | **On or before 31 December 2026** | Institutional brokerage: reconcile custodian net-funds processing. **Not retail-applicable** |

## 4. Payments and collection accounts

| Change | Effective | What the firm must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Every investor-facing collection account must be verifiable on the regulator's verification facility** | **6 September 2026**; new accounts linked **before** collecting | **Link each account to its own validated identifier — even where the firm accepts only bank-transfer rails** |

This one is easy to miss precisely because it feels like a payments-product matter. It is an account-level registration obligation that applies to every collection account, regardless of the rails offered on it.

## 5. Derivatives and risk controls

| Change | Effective | What the firm must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-weekday expiry regime**, one weekly benchmark index option per exchange, minimum one-month tenor for everything else | For contracts expiring **on or after 1 September 2025** | Configure the exchange's chosen weekday; **the two main exchanges differ** — see [the derivatives contract lifecycle](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/products/03-fno-contract-lifecycle/) |
| **Delta-adjusted future-equivalent basis for index option limits**, the new market-wide limit formula, and the ban-period delta test | Phased through 2025, **normal implementation from early December 2025** | Implement the basis **per instrument and per level** — index futures and member-level limits deliberately stayed on notional |
| Separate, higher **intraday** future-equivalent limits for index options | **1 October 2025**, with the expiry-day consequence from **early December 2025** | Monitor intraday against the higher ceilings; an additional deposit applies **only on expiry day** |
| **Order-to-trade ratio framework revised** | **6 April 2026** | Update the option-specific price exemption and designated-market-maker order treatment; load the revised bands |
| **Calendar-spread margin benefit removed on expiry day for single-stock derivatives** | **Three months from 5 February 2026** | **Remove the offset for an expiring stock-derivative leg on expiry day**, preserving eligible non-expiring spreads |
| Daily dissemination of next-day index derivative position limits | **From 6 October 2026** | Ingest the new quantity-limit file; the client-level file moves to **stocks only** from early November 2026 |
| Single-stock derivative lot review | **Phased from late October 2026** | **Import the final exchange contract master. Do not hard-code lot tables** |
| Commodity client position limits and breach penalties revised | **Immediate from 9 September 2026** | Update limits and the **capped** breach penalties, with square-off and escalation controls |
| Commodity early-pay-in margin exemption | **21 September 2026** | Apply the exemption only to qualifying early pay-in; marking obligations remain |
| Commodity winter trading hours | **2 November 2026 to 12 March 2027** | Update session calendars, modification cut-offs and support and treasury shifts |
| Commodity volatility, concentration and near-zero-price parameters | Various, several from **November 2026** | Load the signed annexure values; **some activations are conditional on a separate decision and must not be switched on by notice date alone** |

| Change | Effective | What the firm must do |
|---|---|---|

## 11. Proposals only — not in force

**Nothing in this section may be configured.** Each is a consultation paper as at 15 October 2026, with no final adopting instrument located.

| Area | Proposed |
|---|---|
| **Funding facility** | A higher net worth threshold; eligibility for limited liability partnerships; debentures and other debt as funding sources; a reformulated exposure basis; a cure mechanism for passive client-limit breaches; broader collateral; a thirty-day rebalance on a security leaving eligibility; and reporting before clearing pay-in |
| Client onboarding and identity-agency risk framework | Simplification of onboarding and rationalisation of the agencies' risk-management framework |
| Derivatives ease of doing business | Simplification, clarification and de-merger of clusters of derivatives provisions — **largely a renumbering exercise, which is itself a documentation risk** |
| Market-infrastructure continuity and recovery | Strengthening continuity and recovery at those institutions — **their obligation, not a broker drill frequency** |

A useful signal from the funding-facility consultation: it proposes periodic settlement of excess facility cash, which **already exists** as an exchange requirement. **A proposal restating an existing obligation is not evidence that the obligation is absent today** — and reading it that way is how a firm concludes it may stop doing something it must.

## 12. The change-management discipline

A register decays into a snapshot without a process behind it. This is that process.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| **Daily circular review** | A named owner, every trading day, with a **written record of what was reviewed and what was actioned** — item C-0.12 in [the master trading-day checklist](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/daily-ops/10-master-trading-day-checklist/) |
| Sources monitored | The regulator; both exchanges and the commodity venue; all three clearing corporations; both depositories; the central registry; the financial intelligence unit; and the payments and telecommunications regulators where relevant |
| **Classification on receipt** | Regulation, circular, market-infrastructure change, consolidation, advisory, or **consultation** |
| **Three dates per change** | Issued, **effective**, and the firm's own internal readiness date |
| Impact assessment | Which systems, which configuration keys, which documents, which filings, which roles, which training |
| **Conflict rule** | **Later operative text governs.** Keep the firm's own source and version history; **a consolidation is not authority for the currency of everything in it** |
| Proposal handling | Logged, tracked, **and explicitly not configured**, with a watch for the final instrument |
| Removal handling | Treated as a change: stop the activity, **record it on an abolished list**, and tell the people who were doing it |
| Monthly review | Changes effective in the coming period, with readiness status and owners |
| **Verification pass** | Figures taken from secondary sources are **flagged as placeholders** and re-verified against primary sources before going live |
| Evidence | The review record, the assessments, the configuration changes with approvals, and the training records |

<Aside type="caution" title="The two failure modes of a change register">
The first is **decay**: the register is built during a project, is accurate on the day it is finished, and is never updated — so it quietly becomes a description of a past rulebook while continuing to be cited as current. The defence is the daily review with a written record, because it is the only mechanism that produces a continuous stream of inputs rather than a periodic rebuild.

The second is **conflation**, where proposals, advisories and consolidations are logged in the same stream as operative circulars and eventually treated the same way. This is how a firm configures a consultation proposal, or treats an advisory as a deadline, or assumes a consolidated circular has superseded a later specific one. The defence is classification on receipt — six categories, assigned before anything else happens — which costs seconds and prevents the expensive version of the mistake.
</Aside>

## Practical notes

- **Classify on receipt.** It is the cheapest control on this page and it prevents the most expensive mistakes.
- **Record the daily review even on quiet days.** The record is the evidence the review happened, and the absence of a record on a day a change was published is the finding.
- **Keep an abolished list.** Without it, a removed obligation is revived by the next diligent reader of an old exhibit.
- **Treat secondary-sourced figures as placeholders.** Several items in this register are available only as reproductions, and a placeholder that reaches production is an error waiting for a client.
- **Track vendor deadlines as your own.** A vendor's certification deadline becomes a constraint on the firm's releases.
- **Re-verify the auction-related items first.** They changed in 2026, a reversal decision was pending, and a great deal downstream depends on them.
