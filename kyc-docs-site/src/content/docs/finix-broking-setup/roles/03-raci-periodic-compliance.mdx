> **Why this page is structured this way:** Daily work has natural owners because somebody notices when it does not happen. Periodic work does not: a quarterly return has no symptom when it is missed until a penalty arrives, and an annual register has no symptom until an inspector asks. So this matrix is organised by cadence rather than by function, and section 6 is given to the obligations that are structurally ownerless — which is where periodic compliance actually fails.

## TL;DR

- **One accountable owner per obligation**, and for periodic work the owner must also own the **reminder**, because nothing else will raise it.
- **The compliance officer owns submission, not production.** The desk that produces the data owns its accuracy; compliance owns that it was filed, acknowledged and that the response was read.
- **Three clocks need named owners and are routinely unowned**: the penalty appeal window, which starts on publication; the alert disposal clock; and the audit finding closure clock.
- **Removal is an event with an owner.** When an obligation is abolished somebody must stop the activity, record it, and tell the people doing it.
- **Audit preparation is continuous and owned by the desks**, not assembled by compliance at the last moment.
- **Derive dates from governing calendars.** Several obligations are relative to a published annual calendar, so a fixed-date matrix is wrong for them.

## 1. Conventions

Letters and roles follow [the trading-day responsibility matrix](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/roles/02-raci-trading-day/): **R** responsible, **A** accountable with exactly one, **C** consulted, **I** informed. Roles: **OPS** operations · **TECH** technology · **RISK** risk · **DEAL** dealing · **FIN** finance · **<abbr title="Depository Participant">DP</abbr>** depository operations · **<abbr title="Know Your Customer (process).">KYC</abbr>** onboarding · **COMP** compliance · **CS** client service · **CO** compliance officer · **HOO** head of operations · **HOR** head of risk · **HOF** head of finance · **LEAD** operations lead · **BOARD** board, partners or proprietor.

**A periodic obligation's owner owns its reminder.** Where the matrix names an accountable role, that role is accountable for the obligation being raised in time, not merely for acting once reminded.

## 2. Weekly

| Obligation | R | A | C | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Client-funds monitoring upload | FIN, OPS | **CO** | HOF | LEAD |
| Statement of accounts to clients | OPS | **HOO** | CS | CS |
| Clients mapped to authorised persons | COMP | **CO** | OPS | — |
| Client-code mandatory-field report worked | KYC | **KYC lead** | COMP | CS |
| Override and exception review | RISK | **HOR** | — | LEAD |
| Suspense and break ageing review | FIN, OPS | **HOF** | COMP | LEAD |
| The seven ownerless trading-day items checked | LEAD | **LEAD** | All desk heads | — |

<Aside type="danger" title="Three consecutive misses of the client-funds upload is an external event, not a late filing">
Failure to upload the client-funds monitoring data **for three consecutive weeks** is an enumerated early-warning trigger concerning diversion of client securities. That places it in a different category from an ordinary late submission: it is a signal the regulator watches for, attached to one of the most serious allegations a broker can face. Two consequences for the matrix. The accountable role is the **compliance officer** rather than finance, because the consequence is regulatory rather than operational. And a **second** consecutive miss should escalate to the board rather than to a desk head, because the third one changes the nature of the problem entirely.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Why the compliance officer appears as accountable so often here">
In this matrix the compliance officer is accountable for a large share of rows, which can look like over-concentration. The distinction that resolves it is between **production** and **submission**. The desk that generates the data is responsible for its accuracy and completeness — operations for the balance upload, finance for the net worth inputs, technology for the audit evidence, risk for the margin computation. The compliance officer is accountable for the fact that it was **filed, acknowledged, and that the response was read**, and personally carries the regulatory consequence if it was not.

That split needs stating explicitly in the firm's own documents, because the common failure runs in both directions: a desk assuming compliance will fix bad data before filing, and compliance assuming a desk has checked the acknowledgement. Neither assumption is sound, and the gap between them is where a submission is believed complete and is not.
</Aside>

## Practical notes

- **Give every periodic obligation a reminder owned by its accountable role.** A calendar that nobody owns is a list.
- **Write down the production-versus-submission split.** It is the single clarification that prevents the commonest periodic failure.
- **Review the ownerless list quarterly.** These items have no symptom, so a scheduled look is the only detection.
- **Escalate the second consecutive client-funds miss to the board.** The third one is an external event about client assets.
- **Derive dates from the published calendars** for the obligations that are relative to them.
- **Treat an orphaned accountable role as an incident.** After a reorganisation, an obligation owned by a role that no longer exists is unowned, and nobody will notice.
