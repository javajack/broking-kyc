> **Why this plane exists:** The regulation attaches obligations to functions and then requires that somebody be identifiably responsible for each. It rarely specifies structure. That is why two firms with different organisation charts can both be compliant, and why a firm with an impressive chart can still fail an inspection — the question asked is never "do you have a head of operations" but "who approved this, on what authority, and can they show the certificate that entitled them to".

## TL;DR

- **Accountability is personal and, for several roles, structurally independent.** The compliance officer reports non-compliance to the exchange independently; the money-laundering principal officer must reach senior management or the board.
- **Certification attaches to functions, expires on a three-year clock, and must be revalidated in a window before expiry** — the firm's duty, not the individual's.
- **One accountable owner per activity, always.** Responsibility can be shared; accountability cannot, and shared accountability fails precisely on difficult days.
- **Separate deciding from executing.** The risk desk decides a square-off; dealing places the order; the decision is recorded first.
- **No mandated headcount or shift pattern exists.** The regulation requires adequacy, and the hard clocks do the rest.
- **Two separations everyone assumes are named rules are not** — dealing from risk, and the payout maker from the approver. They are required in substance, and should be presented that way.
