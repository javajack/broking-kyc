> **Why this page is structured this way:** During the session the operation is not executing a sequence; it is watching a board and responding. So the page is built around the board — what is monitored, what each signal means, and what the first response is — followed by the few timed activities that do punctuate the day, then the escalation ladder, then the evening session, which is a different operating mode in the same building.

## TL;DR

- **The session is a monitoring discipline, not a task list.** The board is the artefact: every signal has a meaning and a first response, decided in advance.
- **Three signals matter more than they look**: per-instrument market-data staleness, sequence continuity, and unexpected quiet on a healthy connection.
- **Risk parameter files arrive several times a day** and must be applied within minutes of publication; a missed publication blocks new derivatives orders in that segment.
- **Peak-margin snapshots fall in four random windows** — 11:00 to 11:30, 12:30 to 13:00, 13:30 to 14:00 and 14:30 to 15:00 — which is why margin must be sufficient continuously rather than at end of day.
- **Treasury works to decision points, not deadlines.** The decision must be made early enough to be executed before the bank's cut-off.
- **Pay-in attribution is a client-experience function**, and the published confirmation time is a promise the operation has to keep.
- **The commodity evening session is a second operating mode** running concurrently with the equity end-of-day pipeline.
- **Handovers are written.** Open items at a shift boundary are the ones that become incidents.

## 1. The monitoring board

| # | Signal | Normal | Meaning when abnormal | First response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Session state** per exchange and segment | Open | Halt, extension, or a state the firm did not expect | Follow the state message; message clients by segment |
| M2 | **Sequence continuity** | Continuous | **Divergence already exists** | Stop new flow in that segment; reconcile |
| M3 | Heartbeats | Healthy | Connectivity degrading | Investigate before it fails |
| M4 | Round-trip latency percentiles | Within target | Degradation precedes failure | Investigate; do not wait for an outage |
| M5 | **Per-instrument market-data staleness** | Fresh | **Silent, and poisons every downstream number** | Block price-dependent decisions in scope |
| M6 | Order rate by channel and client | Within profile | Runaway automation, or a channel problem | Throttle the client, never the gateway |
| M7 | **Rejection rate and code distribution** | Stable | A configuration or data problem | Trace the code; broker-preventable rejections are defects |
| M8 | Risk decision latency and timeout count | Within target | Capacity or input problem | Do not raise the timeout as a first response |
| M9 | **Unacknowledged order age** | Zero | Orders in limbo | Investigate; mark unknown rather than assume |
| M10 | Orders in `UNKNOWN` | Zero | Session trouble | Reconcile before the session ends |
| M11 | **Client utilisation distribution** | Stable | Clients approaching square-off | Warning process; see [square-off engine](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/rms/06-squareoff-engine/) |
| M12 | **Firm-level utilisation** | Within ceiling | Correlated client exposure | Risk event requiring a decision |
| M13 | Instrument concentration across clients | Within policy | Everyone on the same side | Consider an instrument-level control |
| M14 | **Risk parameter file freshness** | Current | Under-margined derivatives book | Block new derivatives orders in scope |
| M15 | Collateral and pledge events | Processing | Recognition delays | Investigate; do not recognise unconfirmed pledges |
| M16 | **Pay-in attribution queue** | Clearing | Clients waiting for capacity | Work it; the confirmation time is a promise |
| M17 | Unattributed credits | Zero | Client money unallocated | Age tightly; investigate |
| M18 | Pay-out queue and terminal states | Flowing | Disbursement problems | Check the provider; never blind-retry |
| M19 | **Treasury position against requirement** | Comfortable | A funding decision is due | **Decide at the decision point** |
| M20 | Surveillance alerts | Triaged | Backlog building | Work it; ageing is the metric |
| M21 | Client contact volume and themes | Baseline | An incident the board has not surfaced | Investigate the theme |
| M22 | **Unexpected quiet** | Expected volume | A silent failure | Investigate; healthy components with no traffic is an incident |

<Aside type="caution" title="M5 and M22 are the two that announce nothing">
A stale market-data feed does not disconnect; prices simply stop changing, and every downstream decision — square-off, margin utilisation, band validation, client screens — continues confidently on a number from minutes ago. A quiet connection does not error; it just carries no orders at 10:30. Neither appears on a connection-status dashboard. Both require an expectation to compare against: per-instrument tick age, and a volume profile. They are the cheapest high-value additions to any monitoring board.
</Aside>

## 2. The intraday cycle

| Time | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous | Board monitoring | Operations, risk, technology |
| **Several times a day** | **Risk parameter file publication** — ingest and apply within minutes | Risk |
| On publication | Price band revisions — apply on receipt | Technology |
| On publication | Surveillance list revisions — apply, most restrictive on ambiguity | Risk |
| Intraday | Ban-period list revisions — apply; an underlying can enter during the session | Risk |
| **11:00–11:30, 12:30–13:00, 13:30–14:00, 14:30–15:00** | **Peak-margin snapshot windows** — margin must be sufficient throughout | Risk |
| Continuous | Pay-in attribution and capacity grants | Treasury and operations |
| Continuous | Pay-out processing within the published cut-off | Treasury |
| **At decision points** | Treasury funding decisions, before the bank's cut-off | Treasury |
| Continuous | Surveillance triage | Compliance |
| Continuous | Client support | Support |
| Periodic | **Intraday reconciliation**: order and trade counts, positions | Operations |
| Periodic | Override review | Risk |
| Shift boundaries | **Written handover** | All |

## 3. Risk management during the session

| Activity | Detail |
|---|---|
| Utilisation monitoring | Continuous, with watch and warn thresholds |
| **Warning process** | A shortfall notice with the **amount, the deadline and the consequence** |
| Grace management | Tracked per client; re-evaluate immediately on a confirmed receipt |
| Square-off decisions | Per the disclosed policy; exceptions escalate to a human |
| Mark-to-market monitoring | Clients can breach without placing an order |
| Firm-level utilisation | Approaching a ceiling is a decision, not an automatic rejection of whoever is next |
| Concentration | Across clients in one instrument |
| Override handling | Per-order, logged, by an approver distinct from the placer |
| Restriction changes | Applied immediately; affected clients informed |

## 4. Treasury during the session

| Activity | Detail |
|---|---|
| Position monitoring | Requirement against available, continuously |
| **Decision points** | Alerts before the internal deadline, which is before the bank's cut-off |
| Funding execution | Maker-checker, then send, then **assert recognition** |
| Composition monitoring | Cash-equivalent minimum is continuous, not an end-of-day test |
| Intraday receipts | Confirmed receipts increase capacity; unconfirmed do not |
| Pay-out funding | From the day's clearing corporation receipt, same day |
| Escalation | Early, to the head of treasury and the head of risk |

## 5. Surveillance during the session

| Activity | Detail |
|---|---|
| Real-time alerts | Utilisation spikes, concentration, order-rate anomalies, consecutive rejections, self-trade |
| Triage | Within the service level; **ageing is the metric** |
| Client code modification monitoring | Count by dealer and client, against the daily ceiling |
| Restricted-list enforcement | Employee and connected-person activity |
| Escalation | To compliance; disposition is theirs |
| Recording | Every alert, every disposition, with reasoning |

## 6. The escalation ladder

| Level | Trigger | Owner | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | A board signal outside normal | Operations, risk or technology on duty | Investigate; apply the documented first response |
| L2 | Not resolved within a stated period, or a named incident type | Team lead | Invoke the playbook; communicate internally |
| L3 | Client-affecting, or a regulatory deadline at risk | Head of the function | Client and exchange communication; scoped stops |
| L4 | **Severity one** — order path, client money, a statutory breach | Head of operations and head of risk | Stop trading in scope; notify compliance |
| L5 | Firm-level | Board or designated director | Regulatory notification; external communication |

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Escalate early | The cost of an unnecessary escalation is far below the cost of a late one |
| Named people, not roles | A rota with names and numbers |
| **Compliance informed at L3**, not after | A deadline that will be missed is a different conversation from one that was |
| Record | Every escalation, with times |

## 7. The commodity evening session

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hours | To **23:30**, or **23:55** in United States daylight saving, for non-agricultural commodities |
| Concurrency | **The equity end-of-day pipeline runs while this is open** |
| Staffing | Certified dealing, risk monitoring, support and treasury cover |
| **Tender days** | A named, present, authorised person before **19:30** |
| Treasury constraint | Banking channels closed; client funding options narrow |
| Risk parameters | Evening settings should reflect the client's limited ability to fund |
| Escalation | An evening path with real authority, not best-effort |
| Handover | Into the commodity close, written |

## 8. Handovers

| Boundary | Content |
|---|---|
| Pre-open to session | Open items from the release and the gates |
| Shift to shift | Open incidents, clients under warning, pending treasury decisions, unresolved exceptions |
| Equity close to evening | Commodity positions, tender-day status, treasury position |
| Evening to overnight | Commodity close status, open items, on-call contacts |
| **Written** | Not verbal; the receiving shift signs for it |

## 10. Exception paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Sequence gap | **Highest-priority connectivity incident.** Stop new flow in that segment; reconcile |
| Market data stale for an instrument | Block price-dependent decisions in scope; escalate |
| Risk parameter publication missed | Block new derivatives orders in scope until applied |
| Risk component degraded | **Stop new orders** rather than accept slow decisions |
| Mass client breach in a volatile market | Prioritise by exposure; communicate broadly; escalate early; expect the square-off engine to be a load spike |
| Firm-level ceiling approached | Decision about which flow continues, not an automatic rejection |
| Treasury decision point passed without a decision | Escalate immediately; the bank's cut-off does not move |
| Pay-in attribution backlog | Work it; the published confirmation time is a client promise |
| Surveillance backlog building | Tune with reasoning recorded, or add capacity; do not let it age |
| A client reports something the board did not show | Investigate the gap in the board, not just the client's issue |
| Exchange halts the market | Follow the state message; stop square-off attempts; communicate |

## Practical notes

- **Give every board signal a documented first response.** A board without responses is a wall of numbers at 11:00.
- **Alert on unexpected quiet.** It costs a volume profile and catches failures nothing else catches.
- **Measure per-instrument staleness, not connection health.** Stale is silent and systematic.
- **Escalate to compliance at L3, before the deadline passes.** The conversation is entirely different.
- **Make handovers written and signed.** Verbal handovers lose exactly the items that matter.
- **Staff the evening session properly.** A market open with best-effort cover is an unmanaged position.
- **Do not raise a timeout to fix timeouts.** It is the control, and rising timeouts are a signal.
