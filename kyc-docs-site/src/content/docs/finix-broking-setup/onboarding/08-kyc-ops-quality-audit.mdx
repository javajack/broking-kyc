> **Why this page is structured this way:** The onboarding desk is usually documented as a journey — the steps to open an account — and operated as a queue. What it actually runs is a set of recurring cycles against files that arrive whether or not anyone is waiting for them, and its most consequential output is not new accounts but the continuing permitted-to-trade status of existing ones. So this page covers the cycles first, then the defects, then the quality programme, and gives a section to the traps that cause a firm to disable its own clients.

## TL;DR

- **The desk's main risk is existing clients, not new ones.** Status cohorts and compliance batches silently stop clients trading, and an affected client **cannot even square off**.
- **A fix during the session does not restore trading that session.** Compliance is assessed at a late-evening cut and governs the next trading day.
- **Identifier verification is three-part** — identifier, name and date — and all three must match; corrections to name or date need an unfreeze request and re-verification.
- **Do not resubmit unchanged depository details.** It triggers re-verification and can suspend the client's own trading status — a self-inflicted outage.
- **The status module no longer reverses an inactive status**, so a reactivation workflow built on it stops working silently.
- **Defect rates by source and channel are the real quality metric.** A concentrated rate is an onboarding-process defect, not a records problem.

## 1. The cycles

| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Continuous** | New account processing; modification requests; the three-way client-master reconciliation feeding the begin-of-day gate |
| **Daily** | **Trading-status and not-permitted-to-trade files, in two batches**; the **mobile-number revocation list**; the depository and bank validation status file; **daily deceased-client data** |
| **Weekly** | **The mandatory-field report** — blanks, invalid and incomplete fields including identifier-seeding status |
| **Monthly** | **The identity-agency status cohorts** — on hold, never uploaded, deceased — with their stated cut-offs; client-code particulars |
| On demand | Identifier status reports; the full-file reconciliation, subject to a daily request cap |
| Periodic | Re-verification on the risk-tiered schedule; rescreening; risk-category review |
| Event-driven | Modifications, reactivations, closures, transmission, nomination changes |

## 2. What makes a client permitted to trade

| Requirement |
|---|
| Six identity attributes: **name as per the tax identifier, complete address including postal code for domestic addresses, the tax identifier, a valid mobile number, a valid electronic mail address, and income details** |
| Custodian details where the client uses custodial services |
| **Identifier verification status approved** |
| The client code **active** |
| The tax identifier **compliant with a registration agency** |
| A **valid primary depository account** where applicable |
| Any other field mandatory for the file format and client category |

| Timing rule | Position |
|---|---|
| **The governing cut** | Codes identified as compliant at the **late-evening cut on the previous day** are permitted the next trading day |
| **A fix during the session** | Permits trading **only on the following trading day** |
| Registration-agency compliance | A client becoming compliant is permitted **the next day**, based on the information the exchange receives that day |

## 3. The status cohorts, and why they are a risk item

| Cohort | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **On hold** | **Not permitted to trade — or even to square off — from a stated date.** A client with open derivative positions is **frozen into them** while they move |
| Never uploaded | Not permitted to trade |
| **Deceased** | The code must be marked closed after due diligence, and **all debit transactions blocked** |
| Revoked mobile number | Contact detail invalid, affecting the valid-mobile requirement |

**This is why the cohort files belong on the risk desk's board as well as in the onboarding queue** — see [the periodic and compliance matrix](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/roles/03-raci-periodic-compliance/). The population must be worked before the cut-off, and the risk desk needs to know which clients with open positions are in it.

## 4. The traps

| Trap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Resubmitting unchanged depository details** in a modification file | **Triggers re-verification by the depository and can suspend the client's permitted-to-trade status.** A self-inflicted outage caused by sending correct data. **Include depository details only when they have changed** |
| **Using the status module to reactivate** | **Inactive to active is no longer available** there; the transition must go through a query, a bulk upload or the application interface. A workflow built on the module **stops working with no error naming the cause** |
| Treating the two daily batches as one | The afternoon and late-evening batches reflect different states; the late one governs the next day |
| Assuming a session fix restores session trading | It does not |
| Sharing contact details across unrelated clients | A monitored condition with a penalty basis; the family and authorised-person exception requires a **specific written request** |
| **Capturing the firm's own, employees' or authorised persons' contact details as client details** | Explicitly to be avoided, with a penalty basis |
| Modifying name or date through an ordinary modification | These need an **unfreeze request** and re-verification |

## 5. Defect classes and remediation

| Defect | Remediation |
|---|---|
| Missing document | Obtain; the account cannot be activated meanwhile |
| Unverified document | Complete the verification and record the evidence |
| **Identifier mismatch on any of the three parts** | Unfreeze request, correction, re-verification |
| Inconsistent data across the agency record, the central registry and the firm's master | Determine the correct value, correct at source, propagate |
| Incomplete mandatory field | From the weekly report; correct and re-upload |
| Invalid contact detail | Including a revoked mobile number; obtain and update |
| Depository or bank validation failure | From the daily status file; correct and re-verify |
| Stale periodic re-verification | Complete on the risk-tiered schedule |
| Absent consent or acknowledgement | Obtain; a product may need to be suspended meanwhile |
| **Agency status on hold** | Work with the agency; the client cannot trade or square off meanwhile |
| Screening match | Enhanced due diligence, senior approval or exit per policy, recorded |

## 6. The quality programme

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| **Sampling** | A defined sample per period, weighted toward new clients, new channels and any source with a raised defect rate |
| Checks | Documentary sufficiency; verification evidence; consistency across the three records; the three-part identifier verification; mandatory-field completeness; consent and acknowledgement capture; risk categorisation and its basis |
| **Metrics that matter** | **Defect rate by source and by channel**, first-time-right rate, time to activation, the status-cohort population and its ageing, and the **count of self-inflicted suspensions** |
| **The diagnostic** | **A concentrated defect rate is an onboarding-process defect, not a records problem.** Fix the journey, not the queue |
| Independent review | The internal audit examines the arrangements; a records quality check is conducted as described in [the audit machinery](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/compliance/05-audit-machinery/) |
| Reporting | Defect rates and the cohort position to the compliance officer monthly, with the cohort position also to the risk desk |

<Aside type="caution" title="The metric that tells you the most">
Count the **self-inflicted suspensions** — clients whose trading status was interrupted by something the firm did, rather than by a defect in the client's records. Resubmitting unchanged depository details is the clearest case; using a retired reactivation path is another; a modification routed through the wrong channel is a third.

This number is small, it is entirely within the firm's control, and it is invisible unless counted — the symptom is a client complaint that looks identical to a records defect. A firm that tracks it will find the handful of workflow errors causing most of its onboarding complaints, and each one has a single fix. A firm that does not will keep resolving the same complaint individually, and will attribute it to the agency or the depository rather than to itself.
</Aside>

## Practical notes

- **Work the cohort before its cut-off and tell the risk desk who is in it.** A frozen client with open positions is the firm's exposure.
- **Never resubmit unchanged depository details.** It is the clearest avoidable outage in the plane.
- **Check your reactivation path still works.** The module route was withdrawn and fails silently.
- **Measure defect rate by source and channel.** It converts a queue problem into a journey fix.
- **Count self-inflicted suspensions.** It is the highest-yield metric on this page.
- **Treat the weekly mandatory-field report as work, not information.** It is the cheapest way to prevent next month's cohort.
