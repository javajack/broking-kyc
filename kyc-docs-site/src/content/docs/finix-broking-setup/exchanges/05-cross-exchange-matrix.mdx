> **Why this page exists:** The per-exchange pages explain each market on its own terms. This one exists for the question an engineer or an operator actually asks: given that I built this for NSE, what breaks on BSE or MCX? So it is a difference matrix with a consequence column, ordered from the differences that change architecture down to the ones that change a configuration value.

## TL;DR

- **Three differences change architecture**: BSE's separate connection and protocol instance per segment; MCX's session running eight hours longer with physical delivery; and the second and third clearing corporations with their own files, deadlines, collateral norms and penalty streams.
- **Two concentration days a week, not one.** NSE's weekly index expiry is **Tuesday** and BSE's is **Thursday** following the 1 September 2025 standardisation.
- **Order-type availability is not uniform** and has been narrowing at different times on different exchanges.
- **Price protection differs by segment, not just by exchange.** Limit Price Protection applies in NSE derivatives on top of the operating price range.
- **Everything with a file has three versions.** Three expected-file checklists, three sets of names and timings, three ingestion jobs.
- **Deadlines are per clearing corporation and per bank channel**, so there is no single "settlement deadline" — there is a chain per relationship.
- **The recurring defect is assuming NSE behaviour generalises.** Verify per exchange and per segment; the assumption is what produces the surprises.

## 1. Architecture-changing differences

| # | Difference | NSE | BSE | MCX | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Per-segment connectivity** | Shared stack, segment-enabled | **Separate connection and separate IML instance per segment** | Per MCX architecture | BSE is three integrations: provisioning, monitoring, failover, capacity, certification and runbooks, times three |
| A2 | **Session length** | 09:00–15:40 | 09:00–15:40 | **To 23:30, or 23:55 in US daylight saving** | A second shift, a compressed maintenance window, evening escalation and vendor support |
| A3 | **Settlement medium** | Securities | Securities | **Physical goods** | Warehouse documentation, quality grades, delivery centres — a capability, not a feature |
| A4 | **Clearing corporation** | NSE Clearing | **Indian Clearing Corporation** | **MCX Clearing Corporation** | Three report sets, three collateral regimes, three deadline chains, three penalty streams |
| A5 | **Delivery deadline** | Settlement-day instructions | Settlement-day instructions | **19:30 tender intention on tender days** | A named, present, authorised person on tender days |

## 10. The consequence summary

<Aside type="caution" title="The recurring defect is one sentence long">
"It works on NSE" is the assumption that produces almost every cross-exchange defect in this matrix. The specific failures it causes: one BSE connection provisioned instead of three; a blanket ban-period block that prevents exits; an after-market window that releases into a closed session on a commodity holiday; a single expected-file checklist; one settlement deadline instead of three chains; a session state machine with one close time; and an operating model staffed for a 15:40 close. Each is cheap to prevent by verifying per exchange and per segment, and expensive to discover during certification or, worse, after go-live.
</Aside>

## 11. Verification checklist before assuming parity

Run this per segment before extending a design from one exchange to another.

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Does this segment need its own connection and protocol instance? |
| 2 | What is this segment's session, including any group-specific close? |
| 3 | Does daylight saving or a special session change it? |
| 4 | Which order types and attributes are available **here**, today? |
| 5 | Which price-protection mechanisms apply? |
| 6 | What are the freeze thresholds? |
| 7 | Which restriction treatments exist — ban period, trade-for-trade, call auction, surveillance? |
| 8 | Which clearing corporation, and what is its file set, timing and channel? |
| 9 | What is the acceptable-collateral list and haircut source? |
| 10 | What is the deadline chain, counted back from this bank channel's cut-off? |
| 11 | What is the settlement medium, and does it involve physical goods? |
| 12 | Is a separate certification required? |
| 13 | Does the operating model need different staffing? |
| 14 | Is the client population eligible — for example, NRIs in commodities? |
