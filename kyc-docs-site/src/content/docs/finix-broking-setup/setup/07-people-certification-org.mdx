> **Why this page is structured this way:** Regulatory headcount and operational headcount are two different numbers, and a setup plan that satisfies only the first will not survive the first settlement cycle. So this page separates them explicitly: the named roles and certifications that registration depends on, then the functions that have to be staffed because a deadline exists, then the segregation-of-duties constraints that determine how few people the firm can actually run on, then the shift design that a 23:30 commodity close forces. The authorised-person section comes last because it is a distribution decision that multiplies the compliance surface, and it should be taken after the core organisation is settled rather than alongside it.

## TL;DR

- **Registration depends on specific named people**, not on headcount: at least one designated director resident in India for not less than 182 days in a financial year, a person with at least two years' experience in trading or dealing in securities, a compliance officer, and the NISM certification condition.
- **The NISM certification condition at application is narrower than people assume.** The applicant must ensure that either the proprietor, one designated director or partner, *or* the compliance officer is certified in Securities Market (Basic), Compliance Officers (Brokers), or the relevant module. Segment-specific and function-specific certification obligations then attach separately to the people doing those jobs.
- **NISM Series-<abbr title="—">III</abbr>-A, Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund), is the mandatory compliance certification**; Series-III-B is optional. Certificates are valid for three years, so certification is a recurring obligation with a renewal calendar, not a one-time hire criterion.
- **Segment certification follows the segment**: equity derivatives, currency derivatives and commodity derivatives each have their own module, and depository operations has its own. A firm applying for all segments at once needs all of them covered before those segments are activated.
- **A 23:30 commodity close changes the operating model, not the rota.** Non-agricultural commodities trade to 23:30, or 23:55 during United States daylight saving, with tender-day delivery intentions accepted until 19:30. That is a second shift for dealing, risk and support, plus an end-of-day process that cannot begin at 16:00.
- **Segregation of duties is the real constraint on minimum headcount.** The person who sets a limit cannot approve the change or trade under it; the person who authorises a pay-out cannot be the person who reconciles the bank. Those constraints, not the regulations' named roles, set the floor.
- **The sub-broker category was discontinued in 2018** — existing sub-brokers migrated to authorised person or trading member status by 31 March 2019. Authorised persons are registered by the exchange rather than by <abbr title="Securities and Exchange Board of India">SEBI</abbr>, under a tri-party agreement, with clearer record-keeping and compliance responsibilities than the category they replaced.

## Conceptual overview

There is a persistent gap between how a broking organisation is described in a registration application and how it has to be staffed to work. The application describes roles: a designated director, a compliance officer, a principal officer for anti-money-laundering purposes, certified dealers. The operation needs functions covered at specific times: somebody has to ingest the risk parameter file before the market opens, somebody has to fund the clearing account before the bank's cut-off, somebody has to authorise the pay-out run, somebody has to work the reconciliation exceptions, and on a commodity tender day somebody has to be present at 19:30. Those are not the same list, and the second list is longer.

The binding constraint between them is segregation of duties. A small firm's instinct is to have capable generalists who can do everything, and in a broking firm that instinct produces control failures. The person who can change a client's exposure limit must not be the person who approves the change, because the combination is an unlogged credit decision. The person who initiates a pay-out must not be the one who reconciles the bank account, because the combination is an unmonitored path for funds to leave. The person who places orders must not administer the risk system that constrains them. Each of these pairs forces a minimum of two people, and the pairs do not all overlap conveniently — which is how a firm that thought it needed eight people in operations discovers it needs twelve.

The third factor is certification as a recurring obligation. NISM certificates carry a three-year validity, and continuing professional education exists as a renewal route. For a firm with dealers across four segments, a depository operation and a compliance function, certification becomes a calendar with expiry dates that can silently invalidate a person's ability to do their job. The failure is quiet: nothing breaks, until an inspection asks for the certificate register and finds a lapsed dealer who has been trading for two months.

## 1. Regulatory framework

- **[SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, 2026](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/setup/02-sebi-registration-2026/)** — Chapter <abbr title="—">II</abbr> eligibility including the designated-director residency condition, the two-year experience condition and NISM certification; Chapter III general obligations; Chapter IV fraud prevention including the whistle-blower channel at regulation 25; Chapter VIII code of conduct. `[not yet in index]`
- **NISM certification requirements** — the applicable modules are specified by SEBI and the exchanges from time to time. The applicant must ensure that at any point either the proprietor, one designated director or partner, or the compliance officer is certified in Securities Market (Basic), Compliance Officers (Brokers), or the relevant module. `[not yet in index]`
- **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 and the SEBI master circular on anti-money-laundering** — the principal officer and designated director obligations for reporting-entity purposes. See [<abbr title="Politically Exposed Person">PEP</abbr> and sanctions screening](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/onboarding/04-pep-sanctions-screening/) and the [<abbr title="Financial Intelligence Unit — India">FIU-IND</abbr> circulars index](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/fiu-ind/).
- **Authorised person framework** — the sub-broker category was discontinued in 2018 with migration to authorised person or trading member status by 31 March 2019; authorised persons are registered by the exchange under a tri-party agreement. See [authorised person framework](/broking-kyc/deep-dives/compliance-audit/ap-framework/).
- **[Master Circular for Stock Brokers SEBI/<abbr title="Head Office (SEBI circular ID prefix)">HO</abbr>/<abbr title="Markets Intermediaries Regulation and Supervision Department (SEBI)">MIRSD</abbr>/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/90](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/sebi-mirsd/#sebihomirsdmirsd-podpcir202590)** (17 June 2025).
- **Key management personnel change obligations** — see [<abbr title="Key Managerial Personnel">KMP</abbr> changes](/broking-kyc/deep-dives/member-compliance/kmp-changes/).

## 2. Named roles

| Role | Driven by | Core obligation | Can it be combined? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Designated director** | Chapter II, 2026 regulations | Resident in India not less than 182 days in a financial year; accountability under the regulations | With another board role, yes; the residency condition is personal |
| **Designated director for <abbr title="Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002">PMLA</abbr> purposes** | Anti-money-laundering framework | Accountability for the reporting entity's <abbr title="Anti-Money Laundering">AML</abbr> obligations | Often the same person as above; the obligations are distinct |
| **Compliance officer** | Chapter II and III | Compliance monitoring, reporting, regulatory correspondence, certification condition | **Not** with any operational or trading role |
| **Principal officer, PMLA** | Anti-money-laundering framework | Suspicious transaction identification and reporting, <abbr title="Financial Intelligence Unit">FIU</abbr>-IND filings | Not with trading or client-facing sales |
| **Person with two years' securities experience** | Chapter II | Evidenced experience in trading or dealing in securities | Often a director or the head of dealing |
| **Chief executive or whole-time director** | Governance | Overall responsibility | — |
| **Chief information security officer or equivalent** | Regulation 18(5) and the cyber-resilience framework | Information security programme | Not with the person being audited |
| **<abbr title="Depository Participant">DP</abbr> compliance officer** | Depository regulations | DP-specific compliance | **Separate named role**, not an assumed extension of the broking compliance officer |
| **Internal auditor** | Audit framework | Internal audit of the broking operation | External firm or independent internal function |
| **Grievance redressal officer** | Regulation 10(e), 18(6), investor charter | 21-calendar-day complaint clock, <abbr title="SEBI Complaints Redress System">SCORES</abbr> and <abbr title="Online Dispute Resolution">ODR</abbr> | Within compliance, with capacity |
| **Whistle-blower channel owner** | Regulation 25 | A genuinely confidential channel with retaliation protection | **Must be independent of the people a complaint might concern** |

<Aside type="caution" title="The compliance officer cannot be a part-time operations manager">
This is the most common organisational shortcut in small broking firms and the one that fails fastest in an inspection. The compliance officer monitors the operation; if the same person runs part of the operation, there is no monitoring, only self-reporting. The role can be lean, it can use ComplianceSutra to automate the calendar and evidence, and it can draw on an external consultant for depth — but it cannot sit inside the function it supervises.
</Aside>

## 3. Certification map

Modules and applicability as published by NISM and specified by SEBI and the exchanges from time to time; verify the current list for each function before relying on it.

| Function | Module | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compliance | NISM Series-III-A, Securities Intermediaries Compliance (Non-Fund) | **Mandatory**; Series-III-B is optional | Two hours, 100 marks, 60 percent pass, 25 percent negative marking, three-year validity |
| Application-time certification condition | Securities Market (Basic), Compliance Officers (Brokers), or the relevant module | Mandatory for at least one of: proprietor, a designated director or partner, or the compliance officer | This is the registration condition, distinct from function-level obligations |
| Equity derivatives dealing and sales | NISM Series-VIII, Equity Derivatives | Mandatory for the relevant personnel | Required before the segment is activated |
| Currency derivatives | NISM Series-I, Currency Derivatives | Mandatory for the relevant personnel | — |
| Commodity derivatives | NISM Series-XVI, Commodity Derivatives | Mandatory for the relevant personnel | — |
| Depository operations | NISM Series-VI, Depository Operations | Mandatory for designated DP staff | Needed before DP go-live |
| Operations and risk | NISM Series-VII, Securities Operations and Risk Management | Specified for persons engaged in the relevant functions | Confirm current applicability and scope |
| Research, where offered | Research analyst module | If the activity is undertaken | Not contemplated for Finix at launch |
| Investment advice, where offered | Investment adviser modules | If the activity is undertaken | Not contemplated at launch |
| Continuing professional education | NISM CPE programmes, including exchange-delivered programmes | Renewal route | <abbr title="National Stock Exchange of India">NSE</abbr> delivers NISM CPE programmes for dealers |

| Certification control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Register | One register: person, module, certificate number, issue date, expiry date, the function it authorises |
| Expiry alerting | 180, 90 and 30 days before expiry |
| Access coupling | **System access for a certified function should be gated on a valid certificate.** A lapsed certificate should remove the entitlement, not generate a report |
| Pre-activation gate | A segment is not activated until the certification for it is in place, and the gate is on the certification record rather than on a verbal confirmation |
| Evidence | Certificates retained on the eight-year records floor |

## 5. Segregation of duties — the real headcount floor

| Incompatible pair | Why | Minimum effect |
|---|---|---|
| Limit setting and limit approval | An unapproved credit decision | Two people in risk administration |
| Limit administration and order placement | Self-granted capacity | Risk administration separate from dealing |
| Pay-out initiation and pay-out approval | Unapproved outflow | Two people in the money path |
| Pay-out approval and bank reconciliation | Unmonitored outflow | Reconciliation separate from treasury |
| Client master creation and client master approval | Unverified client | Maker-checker in onboarding |
| Screening alert disposition and client onboarding sales | Conflict of interest | Compliance owns disposition |
| Compliance monitoring and operations | No independent monitoring | Compliance outside operations |
| System administration and transaction processing | Privileged bypass | Technology separate from operations |
| Internal audit and the audited function | No independent assurance | Independent audit |
| Whistle-blower channel and line management of staff | Retaliation risk | Independent channel owner |
| Configuration change and configuration approval | Unapproved policy change | Maker-checker on configuration |

<Aside type="note" title="A workable minimum shape">
Satisfying the pairs above, with a commodity second shift, lands a launch organisation roughly at: a board including the designated director; a compliance officer with an analyst; a principal officer for AML (can sit in compliance if not client-facing); a head of dealing with certified dealers covering two shifts; a risk head with risk administrators and monitors across two shifts; an operations head with settlement, depository and reconciliation staff; a treasury function with at least two people for maker-checker; client support across extended hours; a technology function with separate administration and support; and an independent internal audit arrangement. The exact numbers depend on volume, but the *pairs* are not volume-dependent — they bind at any size.
</Aside>

## 6. Shift design with a commodity segment

| Shift | Hours, indicative | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Early | From about 07:30 | BOD, file ingestion, collateral load, pre-open readiness |
| Day | Market hours to 15:30 | Equity dealing, risk, treasury, support, pay-in |
| Late | 15:30 to 23:45 | Commodity dealing and risk, tender-day intentions to 19:30, commodity session close |
| Post-session | Overlapping the late shift | Equity trade import, reconciliation, contract notes, obligations, upstreaming, pay-out |
| On-call | Overnight | Incident response, batch failures |

| Design point | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Equity end-of-day and the commodity session overlap | Two different operational modes run in the same building at the same time; separate handover documents |
| The commodity close is after the equity end-of-day | Commodity end-of-day is a separate process, later, with its own checklist |
| Tender days have a 19:30 hard deadline | A named, certified, present person on every tender day — not an on-call arrangement |
| United States daylight saving shifts the close to 23:55 | A calendar-driven rota change twice a year, which is easy to forget |
| Risk monitoring cannot be unstaffed while a market is open | A market open with no risk monitor is an indefensible position |
| Handover is a control, not a courtesy | Written handover at each boundary, with open items and their owners |

## 7. Authorised persons and distribution

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Category | The sub-broker category was discontinued in 2018; existing sub-brokers migrated to authorised person or trading member status by 31 March 2019 |
| Registration | Authorised persons are registered by the exchange, not by SEBI — a difference of registering authority, not an absence of registration |
| Agreement | Tri-party agreement between the authorised person, the stock broker and the exchange |
| Obligations | Record maintenance, <abbr title="Know Your Customer (process).">KYC</abbr> procedures, and compliance with SEBI and exchange rules; the responsibilities are clearer than under the predecessor category |
| Broker's exposure | The broker remains responsible to the client for the authorised person's conduct in the broking relationship |
| System support | SecMark's Partner Portal is the stack component for sub-broker and branch network management, commission tracking and partner compliance across broking hierarchies |
| Compliance surface | Each authorised person adds a location, a set of people, a set of client relationships and a monitoring obligation |

<Aside type="caution" title="Do not launch distribution and the core operation in the same quarter">
An authorised-person network multiplies the compliance surface at exactly the moment the core operation is least stable: client relationships created by people the firm does not employ, in locations it does not control, under a monitoring obligation it has not yet exercised. Stand up the direct business first, prove the monitoring, then add distribution. If distribution is strategically essential at launch, cap the number of authorised persons at a number the compliance function can actually visit and monitor.
</Aside>

See [authorised person framework](/broking-kyc/deep-dives/compliance-audit/ap-framework/) for the monitoring obligations in depth.

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Single directory | One identity per person, mapped to per-system roles |
| Joiner | Access granted from the role template, approved, logged |
| Mover | Old entitlements removed before new ones are granted, not alongside |
| **Leaver** | A single checklist spanning all nine systems, completed same day, evidenced |
| Access review | Periodic recertification by the role owner, with evidence |
| Privileged access | Separate, time-bound, logged, reviewed |
| Certification coupling | Entitlements for certified functions gated on a valid NISM certificate |

The leaver case is the one that fails. A departing operations person whose depository and bank-channel access survives their last day is a live control gap, and it is invisible until someone looks.

## 9. Exception paths

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Designated director's days in India at risk | Escalate immediately; it is a registration condition. Plan a second designated director for resilience |
| Compliance officer resigns | A named interim with the certification, and intimation per the KMP change process; see [KMP changes](/broking-kyc/deep-dives/member-compliance/kmp-changes/) |
| NISM certificate lapses for an active dealer | Remove the entitlement immediately, not at the next review; document the period and assess whether trades were placed uncertified |
| Segment activated before certification is in place | Deactivate until certified; the gate should have prevented it |
| Tender-day cover unavailable | Do not take delivery-bound commodity positions into that tender period |
| Single person holds an incompatible pair temporarily | Compensating control, documented, time-bound, approved, and reported to compliance |
| Authorised person non-compliance found | Act under the agreement and intimate the exchange; the broker's responsibility to the client persists |
| Whistle-blower report concerning a senior person | The channel's independence is the whole point; escalate to the board outside the line |

## Practical notes

- **Gate system access on the certificate register.** Reporting on lapsed certificates is a detective control; removing the entitlement is a preventive one, and it costs the same to build.
- **Appoint a second designated director for residency resilience.** One person's travel plans should not put registration at risk.
- **Write the leaver checklist before the first hire.** Nine systems, one list, one owner, same-day completion.
- **Treat the daylight-saving commodity close as a calendar event.** Two rota changes a year, scheduled, with a named owner.
- **Keep compliance out of the operating line, even when it is tempting.** A compliance officer running settlement during a staffing crunch is a finding waiting to be made.
- **Hire the reconciliation function earlier than feels necessary.** Exceptions accumulate from day one, and an exception backlog is how operational problems become regulatory ones.
- **Do not combine the AML principal officer with a sales role.** The conflict is direct and obvious to an inspector.
