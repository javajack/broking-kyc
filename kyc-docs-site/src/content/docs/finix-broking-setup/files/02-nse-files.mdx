> **Why this page is structured this way:** This is the largest file surface a broker integrates with, and it is organised by the eight families defined in [the file register architecture](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/files/01-file-register-architecture/) rather than by portal, because a reader arrives knowing which business process is broken rather than which server the file came from. Two sections exist purely to prevent known defects: section 3 records a format retirement that happened three days before this page was written, and section 13 collects the date-format and naming inconsistencies that have caused real integration bugs. Filenames are reproduced exactly, including the ones that look like typographical errors in the source, because reproducing them exactly is what makes the integration work.

<Aside type="caution" title="How to use the filenames on this page, and what was verified when">
The governing documents are the **annually reissued consolidated circulars** — currently [NCL/CMPT/67751](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt67751) for the cash segment, [NCL/CMPT/61801](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt61801) for futures and options, and [NCL/CMPT/67763](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt67763) for securities lending — together with the file-format standardisation circular [NCL/CMPT/55103](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt55103) and, on the trading side, [NSE/CMTR/57270](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/nse/#nsecmtr57270) and [NSE/FAOP/57264](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/nse/#nsefaop57264).

The detailed nomenclature below was established from the consolidated circulars of the 2023 and 2024 editions and from **direct observation of the venue's own published daily file lists on 15 October 2026**. Names confirmed by live observation are solid. Names taken only from a circular edition that has since been reissued should be re-checked against the current edition before coding, because **the consolidated circulars are replaced every financial year and the earlier editions used here are superseded as documents even where the file names they describe have not changed**. Where this page says a name is unverified, it means exactly that, and the name has not been guessed.
</Aside>

## TL;DR

- **Nothing arrives from one place.** Member-specific files come from the member portal extranet, common files from the public archive, margin reporting through a dedicated clearing application, collateral through another, and compliance submissions through a third. The register records the mechanism per file because the credentials, the network path and the failure mode all differ.
- **The bhavcopy family changed in October 2026.** The legacy `.MS` and `.MD` extranet formats and the broadcast bhavcopy were discontinued on 12 October 2026, and the `.DAT` reports were added to the extranet from July 2026. A greenfield broker must build on the standardised bhavcopy archives plus the `.DAT` files.
- **The margin files are the penalty surface.** Six value-at-risk batches and six or more risk-parameter snapshots per day drive what the broker must collect; the reporting upload and its response file determine whether the broker is treated as having reported at all.
- **The response file is not optional reading.** An upload that was rejected looks identical to one that succeeded unless the response is read. A broker that does not read response files will believe it has reported for weeks.
- **The obligation is not final when you first receive it.** A revised final obligation is published the next day where a custodian did not confirm an institutional trade, under a different filename, and settling on the first version is a pay-in shortfall.
- **Several names contain inconsistencies that must be reproduced, not corrected.** Differing date formats within one family, a delivering-versus-receiving flag whose meaning inverts between two report families, and placeholders with the wrong number of year digits. Section 13 lists them.

## 1. Framework

- **[NCL/CMPT/67751](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt67751)** (29 April 2025) — consolidated circular for the cash segment, current edition. Settlement framework, margin framework, early pay-in, voluntary auction, collateral allocation and release, client-funds upstreaming, file-format standardisation.
- **[NCL/CMPT/61801](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt61801)** (29 April 2024) — consolidated circular for the futures and options segment.
- **[NCL/CMPT/67763](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt67763)** (30 April 2025) — consolidated circular for the securities lending and borrowing scheme.
- **[NCL/CMPT/55103](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/clearing-corps/#nclcmpt55103)** (2 January 2023) — standardisation of clearing and settlement file formats. The source of the standardised naming convention that runs `NCL_<segment>_<report>_<member>_<date>`.
- **[NSE/CMTR/57270](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/nse/#nsecmtr57270)** (26 June 2023) — consolidated trading circular, cash segment. Part D carries the structures of the security master, bhavcopy, trade and order logs.
- **[NSE/FAOP/57264](/broking-kyc/reference/circulars/nse/#nsefaop57264)** (23 June 2023) — consolidated trading circular, futures and options. Part D carries the contract master and spread master structures.
- **NSE/MSD/74764** (18 June 2026) — the bhavcopy dissemination change in section 3. `[not yet in index]`
- **NSE/MSD/60556** (5 February 2024) — standard timings for trade file dissemination. `[not yet in index]`
- Segment consolidated trading circulars for currency and commodity, and the clearing-side currency and commodity consolidated circulars. `[not yet in index]`

## 2. Collection mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it serves | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Member portal extranet, over web** | All member-specific and common extranet files | Separate common and member-specific trees |
| **Extranet application interface over the internet** | Programmatic back-office collection | Parameterised by segment, folder path, date and filename |
| **Extranet application interface over leased line** | The same, for leased-line members | Requires the venue's proxy component |
| **Public archive and the all-reports listing** | Masters, bhavcopy, margin parameters, surveillance and limit files | No credentials; the authoritative live list of public daily files |
| **Clearing interface** | Obligations, custodial confirmation | — |
| **Clearing margin application** | Client margin reporting upload and its response; custodial report download; cash collateral allocation | The margin reporting exchange happens here |
| **Collateral interface for members** | Collateral release and transfer, early pay-in of securities | Upload, response and data-export functions |
| **Electronic member interface for trading members** | Compliance and regulatory submissions, trade annulment | Web forms, not files, for several functions |
| **Trader workstation** | Intraday order and trade backup files written locally | The broadcast bhavcopy that formerly landed here is retired — see section 3 |

**Extranet layout.** Segment roots are distinct per segment, and each has a common folder and a member-specific folder keyed by a segment-prefixed member code. The practical consequence is that a file's full path is a function of segment and member code, so the register stores the path template rather than a literal path, and a member-code change at any point requires a register update rather than a code change.

<Aside type="caution" title="Member-specific and common files fail differently">
A common file that is missing is missing for everybody, which means the venue knows, the problem is usually announced, and waiting is often correct. A member-specific file that is missing is missing only for this broker, which means nobody else will report it and waiting is rarely correct — the usual causes are a credential expiry, a permission change, a member-code mapping error, or a genuine non-generation that needs a call to the venue. Distinguishing the two cases automatically, by checking whether a common file from the same window arrived, turns a twenty-minute diagnosis into an immediate one.
</Aside>

## 3. The October 2026 bhavcopy change

This is the most consequential recent change in the file layer and a greenfield broker must not build on the retired formats.

| Change | Detail | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| **Added** | The `.DAT` bhavcopy reports were added to the extranet, under a bhavcopy folder within each segment's common tree | From July 2026, segment-wise |
| **Discontinued** | Extranet bhavcopy in the legacy `.MS` and `.MD` formats, across cash, futures and options, currency and commodity, including the spread variants | **12 October 2026** |
| **Discontinued** | Bhavcopy delivered over the non-neat broadcast interface, and with it the `.ms`, `.md`, `.yms` and `.ymd` files that the broadcast dropped into the trader workstation report directories | **12 October 2026** |

**What to build on instead:** the standardised bhavcopy archives, which are the primary end-of-day valuation source, plus the `.DAT` interim and final files for early end-of-day processing. Source: NSE/MSD/74764 dated 18 June 2026. `[not yet in index]`

<Aside type="danger" title="This retirement will break back offices that were built before 2026">
Any integration that parses `.MS` or `.MD` extranet bhavcopy, or that depends on the broadcast dropping report files into the trader workstation directories, stopped receiving data on 12 October 2026. For a new broker the lesson is narrower than the change itself: the bhavcopy family has several parallel representations of the same data, maintained for backward compatibility, and parallel representations get retired. Prefer the standardised format that the market-infrastructure data standards drive, treat the legacy and convenience formats as temporary, and record in the register which representation each consumer uses so that the next retirement is a lookup rather than an investigation.
</Aside>

**Mandatory re-upload.** The derivatives contract master must be refreshed on expiry, on a symbol change and after a corporate action, because the corporate action level increments and contracts at the old level are replaced. **Trade-for-trade.** There is no dedicated trade-for-trade list file; the treatment is carried through the **series** and through the settlement type on the clearing side. The authoritative daily feed is the series change file plus the series field in the standardised security master. Any integration looking for a standalone list is looking for something that does not exist.

**Why the interim file matters.** The end-of-day pipeline's chokepoint is trade reconciliation, and the valuation steps that follow need closing prices. The interim file is available materially before the final one, so a pipeline that can start on the interim and re-verify on the final finishes substantially earlier. The design cost is that every valuation step must be idempotent and re-runnable, which it should be anyway.

**Quantity freeze.** Quantity-freeze limits are published and revised monthly and additionally on an ex-date for a bonus, split or rights issue. The path and the revision cadence are established; the **filename is unverified** and must be confirmed before coding.

<Aside type="caution" title="The ban-period file is dated for the next trading day">
The securities-in-ban file published after the close carries the **next** trade date in its name and in its header line, because it tells the broker what will be banned tomorrow. An integration that treats the date in the filename as the file's own business date will apply yesterday's ban list today, which means permitting fresh positions in a security that entered a ban and blocking one that left it. Both directions produce a problem: the first is a penalty, the second is a client complaint. This is the clearest example on this page of why the register separates the *business date* a file is about from the date it arrived.
</Aside>

## 7. Margin parameter files

These drive the broker's own margin engine. They are the inputs without which no margin can be computed, and therefore no margin collected.

| Business name | Filename or pattern | Cadence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk parameter file, end of day** | `nsccl.<YYYYMMDD>.s.spn.gz`; published on the website as a compressed archive | Daily | The standard end-of-day parameter set |
| **Risk parameter file, begin of day and intraday** | `nsccl.<YYYYMMDD>.i<nn>.spn.gz` | **Six files a day**, generated on prices at **11:00, 12:30, 14:00, 15:30, end of day and begin of day** | Intraday margin recomputation; the basis of continuous margin sufficiency |
| Risk parameter file, scaled variant | end of day `nsccl.<YYYYMMDD>.s_1.spn.gz`; intraday `nsccl.<YYYYMMDD>.i<nn>_1.spn.gz` | Same cadence | **Only** for clearing members who opted for same-day mark-to-market settlement. The default uses a different look-ahead. **Using the wrong variant over- or under-margins every client** |
| Risk parameter file, currency | `nsccl_x.<YYYYMMDD>.s.spn.gz` and `nsccl_x.<YYYYMMDD>.i<nn>.spn.gz` | Begin of day, five intraday, end of day | — |
| Risk parameter file, commodity | `nsccl_o.<YYYYMMDD>.{i1..i9,s}` archives | **Begin of day, eight intraday, end of day** | More snapshots because the evening session is long |
| Risk parameter file, debt | `nsccl_d.<YYYYMMDD>.{i1..i5,s}` archives | Begin of day, four intraday, end of day | — |
| **Value-at-risk margin file** | `C_VAR1_DDMMYYYY_N.DAT` where `N` is the batch | **Six batches a day**: batch one at begin of day, batches two to five intraday, batch six at end of day | Value-at-risk plus extreme loss margin for every cash client. Fixed-width with a control record carrying the date and total record count |
| Value-at-risk, lending | `C_VAR1_SLB_DDMMYYYY_1.DAT` | Begin of day | — |
| Applicable exposure limit file | `ael_DDMMYYYY.csv` | Daily | Exposure margin rates per underlying |
| Contract-wise exposure limit | `F_AEL_OPT_CONTRACTS_DDMMYYYY.csv.gz` | Daily | Contract-level exposure margin for options |
| Cash mark-to-market prices | `CM_MTM_Prices_DDMMYYYY.csv` | Daily | Cash mark-to-market and valuation debit |
| Daily settlement prices | `FOSett_prce_DDMMYYYY.csv` | Daily | **Daily mark-to-market settlement of futures.** Without it the mark cannot be settled, client ledgers are wrong and the pay-in amount is wrong |
| Daily settlement prices, currency | `CDSett_prce_DDMMYYYY.csv`; cross-currency `CCRSett_prce_DDMMYYYY.csv` | Daily | — |
| Daily settlement prices, commodity | `CO_sett_prce_DDMMYYYY.csv` | Daily | — |

<Aside type="danger" title="The intraday snapshot cadence is the whole peak-margin mechanism">
Six parameter snapshots a day exist because margin must be sufficient *continuously*, not at day boundaries, and the venue verifies this by comparing what the broker collected against the requirement computed at randomly selected intraday moments. A broker that applies only the end-of-day parameter file is not merely slightly stale — it is computing a different quantity from the one it will be measured against, and the difference becomes a short-collection penalty on every affected client. The applied-state assertion for these files must therefore record *which snapshot* is live, and the begin-of-day checklist must confirm the current one rather than any one. The corresponding operator intervention when a snapshot does not arrive is R-52 in [the RMS operations catalogue](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/rms/11-operations-catalogue/).
</Aside>

## 8. Margin reporting — the penalty-critical exchange

This is a two-way exchange and the only family on this page where the broker's own upload determines whether it is treated as compliant.

| Direction | Business name | Filename or pattern |
|---|---|---|
| broker → clearing corporation | Client margin reporting, trading member | `NCL_CM_MGTM_DDMMYYYY.Mnn`; legacy `C_MRG_TM_<DDMMYYYY>_nn.CSV`; derivatives `NCL_FO_MGTM_DDMMYYYY.Mnn` |

**The upload rules that cause real failures:**

| Rule | Consequence of breaking it |
|---|---|
| Reporting may be submitted during the day and up to a defined number of working days after the trade date, which is the sign-off date | Reporting after sign-off does not count |
| The batch number runs sequentially from the first batch | **An out-of-sequence batch is rejected** |
| The filename must be exactly right | **A wrong filename is silently ignored**, and on the extranet the file is renamed to mark it failed |
| A zero-byte file is rejected | Silent non-reporting |
| The file must end with a newline | **A missing trailing newline breaks processing** |
| The response file always carries a header, whether or not the upload did | Parsers that assume symmetry fail |
| The response must be read and short-reported clients corrected before sign-off | **The broker believes it reported; the penalty says otherwise** |

<Aside type="danger" title="The four silent failure modes in margin reporting">
Margin reporting has the unusual property that every failure mode is silent. A wrong filename is ignored rather than rejected. A zero-byte upload is rejected without anything else happening. An out-of-sequence batch is rejected. A missing trailing newline stops processing partway. In all four cases the broker's own systems record a successful upload, and the only way to learn the truth is to read the response file and reconcile the accepted record count against the submitted one. This is the single strongest argument for the response-file monitoring rule in [file handling exceptions](/broking-kyc/finix-broking-setup/files/08-file-handling-exceptions/): treat an unread response as an unmade submission, alert on it immediately rather than in a daily digest, and reconcile counts rather than trusting a status.
</Aside>

<Aside type="danger" title="The final obligation is published twice and the second one has a different name">
The obligation file received on the trade date is final only if every institutional trade was confirmed by its custodian. Where a custodian rejected or did not confirm, the obligation changes, and the revised file is published the next day under a name carrying an extra infix. A broker that funds and instructs settlement from the trade-date file, and does not collect the revised version, will be short on pay-in day for exactly the amount of the rejected institutional trades — and will discover it at the worst possible moment, because pay-in failure is a default event with penalty and reporting consequences. Two controls: the finality flag inside the file must be checked and an `N` must block settlement instruction, and the revised filename must be a separate register entry with its own expectation on the following day rather than being treated as a re-publication of the first.
</Aside>

## Practical notes

- **Build the register from the venue's own published file list, not from your back office's job list.** The live all-reports listing is the authoritative enumeration of what is available, and it is the only way to find the files nobody is reading.
- **Re-verify against the current consolidated circular each financial year.** They are reissued annually; a register built against an old edition decays silently.
- **Reconcile counts on every response file.** Status fields lie by omission; counts do not.
- **Keep the margin reporting path on its own monitoring.** It is the one family where a silent failure converts directly into a penalty on every affected client.
- **Treat the position file as a daily reconciliation, not a report.** It is the venue's statement of the firm's open positions, and a break in it invalidates margin, mark-to-market and expiry handling simultaneously.
- **Expect the legacy formats to keep retiring.** The October 2026 bhavcopy change is the pattern, not the exception.
